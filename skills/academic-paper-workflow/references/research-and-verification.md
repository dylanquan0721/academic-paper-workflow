# Literature and verification

## Search and screening

Resolve the research question, course scope, language, date limits, and required source types from the brief and user request. Ask only for consequential missing constraints. Generate focused queries with synonyms and appropriate Chinese/English terms; do not assume a required language mix or publication-year cutoff.

Use available web/search tools and authoritative records: publisher pages, DOI records/Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, and relevant disciplinary repositories. Availability and coverage vary; name only services actually queried. Do not claim CNKI/Wanfang/VIP access without having it. If a source needs institutional access, offer to work from a user-provided lawful copy and keep the access limitation explicit. Do not upload private drafts to external services simply to search for literature; use minimal topic queries.

Read primary source records and accessible text rather than trusting search snippets or generated bibliographies. Expand through references/citing papers when the task benefits, without claiming exhaustive coverage. Record actual queries, date, source, selection reason, and rejection reason where material. Do not invent PRISMA counts, independent screeners, databases, or search dates. Call a search systematic only when its recorded method supports that description.

Deduplicate by normalized DOI, then title/authors/year. Relate preprints and journal versions explicitly; generally cite the relevant published version. Retain distinct studies with similar titles. Assign stable IDs such as S001; do not renumber IDs after deletion.

## Evidence ledger

Keep a Markdown table or the project's existing equivalent. Use these fields as needed:

| Field | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| ID and citation | Stable ID, exact title, authors, year, venue, DOI or canonical URL |
| Version and access | Preprint/published/corrected; full text, excerpt, abstract, or metadata only |
| Relevance | Why this paper addresses the research question |
| Evidence | Task, method, sample/data, metrics, findings, and limitations actually stated |
| Locator | PDF page and printed page when different, section/table/figure, or web heading |
| Intended claim | The draft claim this source can support |
| Verification | Existence/metadata status and claim-support verdict separately; date and basis |

Use not reported or not assessed for missing fields. Distinguish author-stated limitations from your own critique. Title-only access can establish metadata or topic, not detailed findings. Abstract-only access may support explicit abstract claims but cannot justify unobserved methodological details or page quotations. Keep direct quotations short and exact; paraphrase faithfully.

## Citation checks

1. **Resolution:** Match every in-text citation to a ledger/reference entry, including each member of grouped citations. Flag unresolved, ambiguous, duplicate, or uncited entries according to the assignment's bibliography conventions. Internal drafts may use `[cite:S001]`; use the required human citation style for deliverables and ensure no internal markers accidentally remain.
2. **Existence and identity:** Consult the original publication or authoritative DOI record and compare title, authors, year, venue, and DOI. Resolve online-first/year and preprint/version differences explicitly. Check known correction/retraction notices when visible. A failed request, missing DOI, or inaccessible page means unverified, not fabricated and not verified. Do not delete a reference solely because a service returned an error.
3. **Claim support:** Inspect the actual cited sentence and accessible source passage. Judge each attributed claim, including table cells and compound claims separately. Record supported, partially supported, contradicted, unclear, or unverified, with a locator and reason. Existence alone never implies support; lack of evidence in an excerpt is not proof of contradiction. Distinguish empirical results, correlational associations, causal interpretations, and the writer's own evaluation.

For corrections, quote or locate the affected draft passage and provide the smallest supported change. Do not silently replace a citation with a different source or strengthen a claim. Recheck edited sentences and the bibliography after authorized revision. Format only checked metadata in the required style; never invent page ranges or DOI values to complete formatting.

Finish with concise coverage: which sources/claims were checked, the evidence basis, unresolved access gaps, and changes made. Avoid calling an entire paper verified when only some references or abstracts were examined. Export BibTeX/RIS only if useful or requested, preserving verified metadata and explicitly missing fields.
